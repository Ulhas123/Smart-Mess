Synthesis & Identified Research Gaps (Transition to Our Approach) • Commercial vs. Campus Non-Stationarity:  Existing demand literature ([1], [2]) evaluates commercial retail with stable patterns; it lacks models for violent institutional calendar shocks (mid-sems, end-sems, semester vacations, festival mass exoduses). • Upstream Attendance vs. Downstream Spoilage:  While food system literature ([3]–[5]) addresses packaging, cold storage, and spoilage, our research tackles the root cause of institutional dining waste: accurate pre-cooking headcount estimation. • Operational Asymmetric Loss:  Traditional models optimize symmetric point errors (MSE/MAE), whereas campus kitchens require calibrated quantile bounds (q10–q90 [9]) and asymmetric penalties to prevent student shortages while minimizing buffer waste.

5 Research Foundations: Continual Adaptation & Synthetic Priors FSNet: Continual Adaptation under Drift (Pham et al., 2022) • Problem Setting:  Online time-series forecasting in non-stationary institutional dining subject to severe concept drift. • Complementary Learning Systems (CLS):  Inspired by human memory; balances fast adaptation to sudden shifts with retention of recurring historical patterns. • Dual-Component Architecture:  Adapts per-layer weights based on gradient EMA while associative memory recalls repeating cyclical regimes. FAST ADAPTER WEIGHT UPDATE (GRADIENT EMA) [Pham et al., 2022 · Ref 6] SLOW MEMORY RETRIEVAL & FORECASTING [Pham et al., 2022 · Ref 6] • Campus Dining Relevance: Models abrupt non-stationary shifts (exam weeks, festival mass departures) alongside recurring cyclical dynamics (weekly menu schedules, weekend drop-offs). Chronos: Synthetic Priors & Foundation Forecasting (Ansari et al., 2024) • Problem Setting:  Severe scarcity of real-world institutional dining card-swipe logs; requires rich synthetic time-series priors. • Language Modeling of Series:  Discretizes real-valued series via mean scaling and uniform quantization, framing regression as categorical classification. • Synthetic Augmentation:  Combines Gaussian Process KernelSynth with TSMixup. TIME-SERIES QUANTIZATION & LOSS [Ansari et al., 2024 · Ref 7] KERNELSYNTH GAUSSIAN PROCESS COMPOSITION [Ansari et al. [7], Rasmussen [12]] • Campus Dining Relevance: Academically justifies synthetic data generation when swipe logs are unavailable; structured stochastic priors enable rigorous benchmarking and stress-testing. Methodological Positioning & Scholarly Distinction • Conceptual Adoption:  We adopt multi-regime drift framing (inspired by FSNet's CLS theory [6]) and domain-informed synthetic benchmark generation (inspired by Chronos [7]). • Model Selection:  While FSNet uses TCNs and Chronos uses Transformers, our deployed engine uses LightGBM [8] with engineered calendar features for exact non-crossing quantile calibration [9], zero tabular overfitting, and sub-millisecond kitchen inference.

9 Technical Contributions: End-to-End Research Pipeline Five-Phase Computational Research Architecture Translates institutional calendar data into validated stochastic simulations and calibrated machine learning forecasts. Phase 0: Calendar Engine • Encodes official IIITA schedules across 2025–2026 (642 calendar days). • Tracks 7 distinct regimes:  Mid-Sem, End-Sem, Breaks, Summer, Pre-Sem, Holidays, Normal. • Generates anticipatory counters:  days_to_next_exam and days_since_exam. Phase 1: Mess Menu Modeling • Granular meal schedules for BH-2 (Pure Veg) and BH-3 (Non-Veg hostel). • Multi-hot dish indicator vectors for special items, desserts, and fast food. • Encodes binary non-veg dinner availability flags (Monday & Friday in BH-3). Phase 2: Stochastic Attendance • Synthesizes 11,718 observations (642 days × 2 hostels × 3 meal slots). • Parameterized Heteroscedastic Negative Binomial:  Headcount ~ NegBin( μ, α ₜ) [Cameron & Trivedi, 2013 [11]]. • Dynamically modulates headcount via calendar, menu, weather, and festival kernels [Hawkes [12]]. Phase 3: Simulator Validation & Sweeps • Parameter Identifiability:  OLS regression on log-attendance successfully recovers true simulation multipliers without bias ( β_ exam ≈ 1.058,  β_ break ≈ 0.152) [Cameron & Trivedi [11]]. • Robustness Grid Sweeps:  Evaluated across 9 noise dispersion ( α₀)  and multiplier shock regimes; error distributions remain stable and monotonic. • Methodological Boundary:  Confirms mathematical consistency of generator prior to machine learning modeling. Phase 4: ML Forecasting & Evaluation • Multi-Scale Feature Engineering:  25+ features spanning calendar flags, weather metrics, menu items, autoregressive lags (1, 2, 7, 14), and rolling stats (7d, 14d). • Quantile LightGBM Forecaster:  Jointly predicts median point forecasts (q₅₀) and calibrated 90% prediction intervals (q₁₀, q₉₀) via pinball loss [Koenker & Bassett, 1978 [9]]. • Benchmark Evaluation:  Rigorous chronological test evaluation (612 meal events) against Seasonal Naive, Holt-Winters, and SARIMA baselines.

10 Synthetic Data Generation: Domain Mechanics & Stochastic Engine Domain Multipliers & Simulation Mechanics • Dataset Scope:  642 calendar days (Jan 2025 – Oct 2026), 2 hostels, 3 slots = 11,718 rows. • Hostels & Capacities:  BH-2 (300 cap, pure veg) & BH-3 (300 cap, non-veg). • Base Slot Attendance Ratios:  Breakfast (0.42), Lunch (0.67), Dinner (0.76). • Academic Calendar Multipliers:  End Break (0.15), Mid Break (0.35), Summer Sem (0.30), Pre-Sem (0.25), Exam Period (1.06), Gazetted Holiday (0.82). • Weather Sensitivity:  Rain breakfast (0.82), rain lunch (0.96), extreme heat ≥40°C (0.97), cold ≤10°C (0.98). • Menu Pull Factors:  Dessert (+6%), Fast Food (+5%), Special Item (+4%), Non-Veg (+7% on Mon/Fri dinner in BH-3). EXPONENTIAL FESTIVAL DECAY KERNEL [Hawkes, 1971 [12]; Khan & Erdoğdu [1]] Stochastic Noise Formulation STOCHASTIC DISTRIBUTION & EXPECTED HEADCOUNT [Cameron & Trivedi, 2013 [11]] HETEROSCEDASTIC DISPERSION (VARIANCE INFLATION) [McCullagh & Nelder, 1989; Ref 11] • Behavioral Variance: Reflects heightened student behavioral volatility and departure uncertainty during examinations and festival transitions. CRITICAL ACADEMIC DISCLOSURE • No Real Student Attendance Logs:  No actual IIIT Allahabad student attendance records were available or utilized in this work. • Synthetic Simulation Methodology:  All 11,718 observations are computer-generated using the calibrated Negative Binomial stochastic generator. • Domain Calibration Targets:  Base attendance rates and multipliers represent heuristic estimates derived from campus mess staff interviews. • Evaluation Scope:  Metrics assess the machine learning model's ability to learn and recover structured non-stationary dynamics, not real-world dining accuracy.

16 Empirical ML Implementation: Features & LightGBM Pipeline Engineered Feature Representation (Phase 4a) • Calendar & Regime Features (Primary Signal):  Days into semester, days to upcoming exam, days since last exam, binary regime flags (is_exam, is_break, is_holiday, is_weekend), day-of-week, month. • Weather Exogenous Signals:  Max and min temperatures, precipitation (mm), and binary rainy-day indicator. • Menu Signals & Event Kernels:  Special food items, desserts, fast-food flags, BH-3 non-veg dinner, and continuous festival exponential decay kernel. • Autoregressive Lags & Rolling Stats:  Lags 1, 2, 7 (same slot last week), 14; 7d and 14d rolling means and standard deviations. • Contextual Encodings:  Categorical encoding for hostel (BH-2/BH-3) and meal slot (Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner). Data Leakage Prevention & Horizon Rigor • Strict Causal Boundary:  Uses strictly backward-looking lags (t-1, t-2, t-7, t-14) and verified institutional calendar schedules; zero future lookahead leakage. • Tree-Based Partitioning:  Decision trees naturally isolate conditional regime conjunctions (e.g. rain AND mid-sem break) without manual interaction expansions. Why LightGBM Forecaster? (Phase 4b) • Tabular Superiority:  Gradient-boosted decision trees consistently outperform deep neural networks on tabular series with sharp calendar regime boundaries. • Non-Linear Regime Interactions:  Decision trees naturally partition conditional conjunctions (e.g. rain AND mid-sem break) without combinatorial feature explosion. PINBALL LOSS OBJECTIVE FOR QUANTILE FORECASTING [Koenker & Bassett, 1978 [9]; Ref 8] • Non-Crossing Quantile Heads:  Efficient pinball loss optimization provides calibrated 90% prediction intervals (q₁₀ to q₉₀) without quantile crossing artifacts. • Computational Efficiency:  Sub-millisecond inference time enables seamless deployment on low-cost kitchen tablets without cloud GPU requirements. • Baselines for Comparison:  Evaluated against Seasonal Naive, Holt-Winters Exponential Smoothing, and SARIMA.

Conclusion, Academic Limitations & Future Work Contributions Delivered • Multi-Regime Simulator:  High-fidelity synthetic generator (11,718 rows) capturing calendar, weather, menu, and festival kernels. • Quantile LightGBM Engine:  Robust forecasting reducing MAE by 39.1% over Seasonal Naive and outperforming SARIMA/Holt-Winters. • Validated Feature Pipeline:  Empirically established the critical importance of academic calendar features (+5.74 MAE penalty when removed). • Operational Calibrations:  Quantile prediction intervals (PICP 72.4%) capturing 67% of meals within ±50 portions. Academic Limitations • Synthetic Attendance Nature:  No actual student swipe-card data was available or used; baseline attendance rates are assumed placeholder targets. • Internal vs Real Generalization:  Simulator validation confirms mathematical consistency, not real student behavioral fidelity. • Unmodeled Dynamics:  Student peer group clustering, unexpected off-campus food ordering, and localized food preferences are not modeled. Future Work Roadmap • Empirical Telemetry Collection:  Deploy student QR scanners in BH-2 and BH-3 to capture real ground-truth attendance logs. • Domain Adaptation & Transfer:  Fine-tune synthetic pretrained models using real student attendance streams. • Edge Computer Vision:  Integrate CV models at dish return counters for automated plate-waste estimation. • Nutritional Optimization:  Multi-objective menu planning balancing nutrition, cost, and ingredient fatigue. 21
